# Analysis of Racial Disparities in Policing and Prosecution Decisions
## Orange County, California (2021-2024)

---

## Executive Summary

This analysis examines racial disparities in discretionary decision-making across two stages of the criminal justice system: policing and prosecution. The key research question is: **How do racial disparities in discretionary police decisions compare to racial disparities in prosecutorial decision-making?**

The findings reveal substantial racial disparities at both stages, with particularly pronounced differences in policing decisions (stops and searches) compared to more modest but still significant disparities in prosecutorial charging decisions.

---

## I. Policing Analysis: Stop and Search Disparities

### Table 1: Stops and Searches per 1,000 Residents (2022-2024)

**Key Findings:**

1. **Stark Disparities in Stop Rates:**
   - Black/African American residents experience by far the highest stop rates: **190-197 stops per 1,000 residents** across all three years
   - This is **3.5-4.1x higher** than White residents (46-55 per 1,000)
   - Hispanic/Latino residents also face elevated rates: **63-78 per 1,000** (1.2-1.5x White rates)
   - Asian residents have the lowest rates: **27-32 per 1,000** (approximately half the White rate)

2. **Search Rate Disparities Mirror Stop Patterns:**
   - Black/African American residents: **16-19 searches per 1,000** (highest)
   - Hispanic/Latino residents: **6.4-8.5 searches per 1,000**
   - White residents: **2.9-3.6 searches per 1,000**
   - Asian residents: **0.77-0.83 searches per 1,000** (lowest)

3. **Population Context:**
   - Despite comprising only **1.6%** of Orange County's population (49,304), Black residents account for a disproportionate share of police stops
   - Hispanic/Latino residents make up **35.2%** of the population (1,086,834)
   - White residents: **38.8%** (1,198,655)
   - Asian residents: **22.6%** (699,124)

**Takeaway:** Police contact rates show dramatic racial disparities, with Black residents experiencing police stops at rates that cannot be explained by population proportions alone. The consistency across all three years suggests systemic patterns rather than random variation.

### Table 2: Search Rates and Hit Rates by Race (2022-2024)

**Key Findings:**

1. **Search Rates (% of stops resulting in search):**
   - Hispanic/Latino: **9.85-10.80%** (highest)
   - Black/African American: **9.39-9.93%**
   - White: **6.24-6.55%**
   - Asian: **2.52-2.85%** (lowest)
   - The gap between Hispanic/Latino and White search rates is approximately **40-65%** higher

2. **Hit Rates (% of searches finding contraband):**
   - **Inverse relationship:** Groups with higher search rates have *lower* success rates
   - White: **34.12-38.19%** (highest hit rate)
   - Asian: **30.47-32.41%**
   - Hispanic/Latino: **26.16-31.01%**
   - Black/African American: **24.67-28.60%** (lowest hit rate)

3. **The "Search Rate-Hit Rate Paradox":**
   - Despite being searched most frequently, Black and Hispanic/Latino individuals are **least likely** to be found with contraband
   - White individuals, despite being searched less often, have the **highest** hit rates
   - This pattern is consistent across all three years

**Takeaway:** The data reveals potential evidence of outcome bias in search decisions. The negative correlation between search rates and hit rates by race suggests that officers may be applying different evidentiary thresholds when deciding whom to search. Higher search rates combined with lower hit rates for Black and Hispanic/Latino individuals indicate searches may be conducted with weaker probable cause for these groups.

## II. Prosecution Analysis: Charge Enhancement Disparities

### Table 3: Enhancement Rates by Race, Statute Level, and Year (2021-2023)

**Key Findings:**

1. **Felony Enhancements (generally very low rates):**
   - Hispanic/Latino: **1.18-2.08%** (highest)
   - Black/African American: **0.18-0.83%**
   - White: **0.41-0.57%**
   - Asian: **0.08-0.37%** (lowest)
   - Most confidence intervals overlap, suggesting differences may not be statistically significant

2. **Misdemeanor Enhancements (much higher rates):**
   - Asian: **14.27-17.47%** (highest, surprisingly)
   - Other: **13.62-16.61%**
   - Hispanic/Latino: **12.12-13.38%**
   - Black/African American: **9.88-11.21%**
   - White: **9.14-11.49%** (lowest)

3. **Temporal Trends:**
   - Felony enhancement rates generally declining from 2021 to 2023 for most groups
   - Misdemeanor enhancement rates more stable, with some year-to-year variation

**Important Context:**
- "Enhancements" refer to additional charges that increase severity/penalties
- These are discretionary decisions made by prosecutors
- Unlike policing data, enhancement rates for Asian defendants are unexpectedly high in misdemeanor cases

**Takeaway:** Prosecutorial disparities are more complex and modest than policing disparities. For felonies, Hispanic/Latino defendants face slightly elevated enhancement rates. For misdemeanors, the pattern reverses, with Asian defendants experiencing highest rates. This may reflect different case compositions, charging practices for specific offense types, or other confounding factors not controlled for in this analysis.
